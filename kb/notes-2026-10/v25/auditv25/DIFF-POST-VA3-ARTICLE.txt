\begin{abstract}
In Penrose's Conformal Cyclic Cosmology (CCC) the future conformal boundary $\scri$ of one aeon is identified with the initial singularity of the next, and the rescaled matter fields are required to pass through it. We study a test Dirac field of mass $m$ on de Sitter space and on spatially flat FLRW backgrounds that reach $\scri$ in finite conformal time, rescaled with the standard conformal weight $3/2$. (i) Every non-zero mode keeps a constant norm and acquires the phase $\exp(\pm\ii\int m\,\dd t)$, so that the rescaled field has no limit at $\scri$; this asymptotic form is known, and we read it at $\scri$. On de Sitter space, in conformal time, the mode equation has a regular singular point at $\scri$ with exponents $\pm\ii m/H$. (ii) For solutions that contain, at $\scri$, only one of the two exponents (late-time particle or late-time antiparticle solutions), the winding is a common phase which a $U(1)$ gauge transformation of a charged field removes, at the price of a Maxwell potential with a non-integrable pole; for solutions that contain both, the relative phase is gauge invariant. There is no gauge in which both the rescaled field and the potential extend continuously. (iii) With the radiation weight $\Omega^{-4}$, the rescaled energy density behaves as $m(\|\chi_{0}^{+}\|^{2}-\|\chi_{0}^{-}\|^{2})/\Omega$; the coefficient has no fixed sign and may vanish. With the dust weight $\Omega^{-3}$ used by Friedrich for pressure-free matter the same density [-is regular:-]{+has a limit:+} the pole belongs to the choice of variable, while the interference between the two exponents does not. For a one-particle excitation of a pure quasi-free Hadamard state the pole statement holds for the expectation value relative to the state, integrated over a comoving slice; its coefficient depends on the state. (iv) For a time-dependent mass we give three strictly ordered conditions ($\int^{\infty}m\,\dd t<\infty$; $m\,a$ bounded; $m=\Omega\mu$ with $\mu$ smooth). We state the consequences for CCC as conditions on a chosen matching rule, not as an exclusion of every matching.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Conformal Cyclic Cosmology \cite{Penrose2010,Tod2015,MeissnerPenrose2025} identifies the future conformal boundary $\scri$ of an asymptotically de Sitter universe with the initial singularity of a subsequent one. The construction requires the matter content, conformally rescaled, to pass from one aeon to the next. Massless conformally invariant fields behave well under the rescaling; massive fields do not, and Penrose postulates that rest mass fades or becomes negligible near $\scri$ \cite{Penrose2010}. Tod lists the implementation of this ``mass fade-out'' among the open questions of CCC, and notes that without such a mechanism pressure-free matter, with density proportional to $a^{-3}$, comes to dominate radiation \cite{Tod2022}. This paper asks what, precisely, a massive Dirac field does at $\scri$, and what would have to happen to its mass for the field to be regular there.

\paragraph{Related work.} The mode functions of the massive Dirac field in the flat chart of de Sitter space are Hankel functions of order $\tfrac12\mp\ii m/H$ \cite{Cotaescu2002}. Their late-time form, a plane wave $e^{\mp\ii mt}$ in each eigenspace of $\gamma^{0}$ plus an exponentially small error, is Lemma~3.1 of Dappiaggi, Finster, Murro and Radici \cite{DFMR2019} (after Finster and Reintjes \cite{FinsterReintjes2016}); Ibison \cite{Ibison2011} describes the infinitely fast phase oscillation at the future conformal boundary and the growth, in the conformal frame, of the rest energy of a free electron. The fermionic pair-creation factor $1/(e^{2\pi m/H}+1)$ is standard \cite{Boonpan2026}. Ibison's conclusion that only one eigenspace of $\gamma^{0}$ reaches the boundary holds, for positive-frequency modes, up to [-terms of-]{+a+} relative [-size-]{+weight+} $e^{-2\pi m/H}$; for general solutions both components persist (Proposition~\ref{prop:dirac}). For a massive scalar field in CCC, Bachelot \cite{Bachelot2020} gives integral conditions on a decaying mass under which the rescaled field crosses the bang surface; Friedrich \cite{Friedrich2015} shows that the coupled Einstein--massive-scalar system is regular at $\scri$ when $m^{2}=2\lambda/3$, and notes that each massive field needs its own analysis; for Einstein--$\lambda$--dust he constructs an open set of solutions with a smooth $\scri$ \cite{Friedrich2017}. Conformal scattering has been developed for the massless Dirac field on Kerr space-time \cite{Pham2021}; see also \cite{MasonNicolas2004} on conformal scattering and \cite{MasonNicolas2012} on the peeling of Dirac and Maxwell fields on a Schwarzschild background; the conformal extension of the Bunch--Davies state across the de Sitter boundary has been studied for the scalar field \cite{Wrochna2017}. On the CCC side, Markwell and Stevens list the asymptotic decay of rest mass among the controversial assumptions of CCC that they do not address, and state Penrose's ``suppressed rest-mass hypothesis'' as a condition on the conformal factor \cite{MarkwellStevens2022}, after \cite{GurzadyanPenrose2013}. Proposition~\ref{prop:dirac} below is therefore a reading at $\scri$ of a known asymptotic form; the classical core of Proposition~\ref{prop:energy} is in \cite{Ibison2011}; Proposition~\ref{prop:varmass} is the Dirac counterpart of \cite{Bachelot2020}, with a different exponent; we have not found Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge}. The search is not exhaustive and we make no priority claim.

\paragraph{Results.} Proposition~\ref{prop:dirac} (Section~\ref{sec:modes}) gives the structure of every mode at $\scri$, and Remark~\ref{rem:fuchs} its Fuchsian form. Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge} (Section~\ref{sec:gauge}) separates what depends on the gauge from what does not. Proposition~\ref{prop:energy} (Section~\ref{sec:energy}) gives the rescaled energy density and its quantum version. Lemma~\ref{lem:coupling} (Section~\ref{sec:coupling}, Appendix~\ref{app:basis}) shows that local couplings to the geometry, within a stated class, only redefine a constant mass. Proposition~\ref{prop:varmass} (Section~\ref{sec:varmass}) gives the conditions on a time-dependent mass. Remark~\ref{rem:friedrich} summarises the formal regularity of the coupled [-systems, whose-]{+systems and, for a test field, the behaviour of the geometric sources on solutions; the+} computations are in a companion note \cite{Companion}. Section~\ref{sec:ccc} states what this implies for CCC under explicit matching rules, and Section~\ref{sec:scope} lists what is not established.

On de Sitter space every such tensor is a polynomial in $g_{ab}$ and $\epsilon_{abcd}$ with constant coefficients: the Riemann tensor is $H^{2}(g_{ac}g_{bd}-g_{ad}g_{bc})$ and all its covariant derivatives vanish. A basis of the Clifford algebra is $\{1,\gamma_{5},\gamma^{a},\gamma_{5}\gamma^{a},\sigma^{ab}\}$, and the invariant tensors of rank $0,1,2,3$ are, respectively, constants, none, $g_{ab}$, none (rank 4: $g_{(ab}g_{cd)}$-type products and $\epsilon_{abcd}$).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item No derivative. Rank 0: $\bar\psi\psi$, $\bar\psi\gamma_{5}\psi$. Rank 1 ($\gamma^{a}$, $\gamma_{5}\gamma^{a}$): no invariant vector. Rank 2 ($\sigma^{ab}$): $g_{ab}$ is symmetric and $\sigma^{ab}$ antisymmetric, so the contraction vanishes; $\epsilon_{abcd}\sigma^{ab}\propto\gamma_{5}\sigma_{cd}$ leaves two free indices.
\item One derivative $\nabla_{c}$. With $\Gamma=1$ or $\gamma_{5}$: no invariant vector to contract $c$. With $\gamma^{a}$ or $\gamma_{5}\gamma^{a}$: $g_{ac}$ gives $\gamma^{a}\nabla_{a}$ and $\gamma_{5}\gamma^{a}\nabla_{a}$. With $\sigma^{ab}$: a rank-3 invariant would be needed; there is none.
\item Two derivatives that do not raise the order of the equation. The terms [-$\nabla_{a}\bar\psi\,\sigma^{ab}\nabla_{b}\psi$-]{+$\ii\nabla_{a}\bar\psi\,\sigma^{ab}\nabla_{b}\psi$+} and $\nabla_{a}\bar\psi\,\gamma_{5}\sigma^{ab}\nabla_{b}\psi$ {+(real as written)+} give no second-order term, since $\sigma^{ab}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}=\tfrac12\sigma^{ab}[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}]$. Up to a total derivative they equal $-\tfrac12\bar\psi\,\sigma^{ab}[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}]\psi$ (resp.\ with $\gamma_{5}$), i.e.\ by the Ricci identity a constant multiple of $\bar\psi\,\sigma^{ab}R_{abcd}\sigma^{cd}\psi\propto H^{2}\bar\psi\psi$ (resp.\ $\bar\psi\gamma_{5}\psi$) on de Sitter space: constant mass terms.
\end{itemize}
Products of several gamma matrices reduce to this basis. Hermiticity of the action makes the four coefficients real (with $\ii$ factors as written in the proof), and terms with the derivative acting on $\bar\psi$ differ from these by total derivatives. The resulting equation is $\big[(Z_{1}+Z_{2}\gamma_{5})\ii\gamma^{a}\nabla_{a}-(m+\ii m_{5}\gamma_{5})\big]\psi=0$ with constants $Z_{1},Z_{2},m,m_{5}$.
