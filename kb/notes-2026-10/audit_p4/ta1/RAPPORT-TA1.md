## Section Q1 : quelle source construit un raccord a → ∞ (de Sitter asymptotique) vers un nouveau Big Bang (a → 0) ?

Réponse globale : **aucune des six sources ne construit une solution des équations d'Einstein avec matière qui relie une expansion infinie asymptotiquement de Sitter (a → ∞) à un nouvel univers de type Big Bang (a → 0 d'un second univers).** Aucune ne mentionne l'infini futur (scri plus, future infinity) comme lieu de raccord, et aucune ne mentionne la cosmologie cyclique conforme de Penrose. Le seul endroit où l'expression « future infinity » apparaît est la source 3, dans un contexte holographique sans raccord (voir ci-dessous). Détail par source :

### Source 1 (Gielen, Turok) : cas (a), rebond à travers a = 0 du même univers

Nature de la solution : univers plat rempli de radiation (plus scalaires conformes), a(η) ∝ η, sans constante cosmologique. Le rebond passe par a = 0 (en fait autour de a = 0 dans le plan complexe).

- p. 1 : « A simple example is a spatially flat, homogeneous, and isotropic universe filled with a perfect radiation fluid, with line element ds² = a²(η)(−dη² + dx²), (1) with a(η) ∝ η, where η is the conformal time. This is a good metric for all η ≠ 0, and furthermore possesses a unique analytic continuation around the singularity in the complex η-plane. »
- p. 2 : « it is convenient to define the Einstein-frame scale factor a by x^α = av^α with v an (M + 1)-vector satisfying v² = −1, so that real negative and positive values of a represent the two “gravity” regions, while imaginary values of a represent the “antigravity” region. We shall study generic complex solutions by analytically continuing a into the complex plane and around the singularity at a = 0. »
- p. 4 : « As a runs in from −∞ and approaches the origin, it turns down the imaginary a-axis to a = −i√(−C)/m before heading back to the origin and out to +∞. »

Il s'agit d'un passage a → −∞ … 0 … +∞ (a négatif puis positif, deux régions « gravity »), pas d'un raccord a → +∞ vers a → 0. Pas de constante cosmologique positive, pas de phase de Sitter. Aucune mention de l'infini futur ni de Penrose. La source ne dit rien sur (b), (c), (d).

### Source 2 (Feldbrugge, Lehners, Turok) : cas (b), création « à partir de rien » (condition sans bord q₀ = 0 vers un univers de Sitter)

- p. 22 : « To implement “no-boundary” conditions, we must take q₀ = 0 and find a 4-metric which is regular there. This is possible for positive k. [...] We will take the final boundary to correspond to a late time configuration, where the universe has become large, q₁ > 3/Λ. »
- p. 5 : « The classical saddle point solution is just a four-sphere, continued at its equator to de Sitter spacetime. »

Les auteurs traitent aussi (p. 21) le propagateur entre deux bords classiques, avec deux solutions de Sitter dont l'une traverse le col (« waist ») de l'hyperboloïde : « For classical boundary conditions there exist two solutions: either q₀, q₁ both sit on the same side of the waist of the de Sitter hyperboloid, or they are separated by the waist. » Cela reste un même espace de Sitter, sans a → 0.

Aucune mention de a → ∞ comme lieu de raccord, de l'infini futur, ni de Penrose. Le modèle est le minisuperespace fermé avec Λ > 0 ; le propagateur est calculé jusqu'à une surface finale q₁ finie. La source ne dit rien sur (c) et (d).

### Source 3 (Díaz Dorronsoro, Halliwell, Hartle, Hertog, Janssen) : cas (b), avec deux copies renversées dans le temps

- p. 2 : « Specifically, with no-boundary conditions this yields the usual, real no-boundary wave function describing two identical copies of an ensemble of asymptotically classical, inflationary universes [15, 16] with Gaussian fluctuations [17]. »
- p. 9 : « The wave function is real and describes in this classical λ ≫ 1, φ₁ ≪ 1/√λ domain two identical copies of an ensemble of slow roll inflationary universes that are asymptotically de Sitter [15, 16, 26]. These classical ensembles can be viewed as the time-reversal of each other. [...] If one thinks of one set of histories as expanding, in the other set they are contracting. Individual histories in both ensembles are not connected classically but may be connected by quantum evolution mediated by the Wheeler-DeWitt equation [27, 28]. »

Les univers sont « asymptotically de Sitter » (donc a → ∞ dans le futur de chaque histoire), mais il n'y a aucune construction de solution reliant ce a → ∞ à un a → 0 d'un second univers. Le seul raccord évoqué est quantique, entre une branche contractante et une branche en expansion, via l'équation de Wheeler-DeWitt, et il est renvoyé à d'autres travaux [27, 28].

Mention de l'infini futur : p. 12 : « A promising approach towards this is to use holographic techniques which might enable one to express the wave function of backgrounds and fluctuations in terms of the partition functions of a set of deformations of Euclidean CFTs defined on the future boundary. The reversal of the physical arrows of time in the histories predicted by the no-boundary wave function means it is conceivable that a single dual defined at future spacelike infinity indeed encodes all physical correlations. » Il s'agit d'un bord holographique, non d'un raccord vers un nouvel univers. Aucune mention de Penrose ni de CCC.

### Source 4 (Feldbrugge, Lehners, Turok) : cas (b), création « à partir de rien »

- p. 1 : « In this Letter, we analyze two intriguing and long-standing proposals, due to Hartle and Hawking [1, 2] and Vilenkin [3, 4], respectively HH and V, describing the quantum creation of universes using the gravitational path integral. »
- p. 3 : « The path integral to evolve from a(0) = 0 to a(1) = a₁ is [10, 11] G^{(0)}[a₁; 0] = ∫_{0⁺}^{∞} dN ∫_0^{a₁} Da e^{iS^{(0)}[a,N]/ħ}. »
- p. 4 : « Furthermore, we assume this classical evolution results in a smooth “bounce” of the scale factor such as occurs in the closed slicing of de Sitter spacetime. »

Le « bounce » cité est le rebond de de Sitter fermé à son rayon minimal (q_B = 3/ρ), non un passage par a = 0 ; la solution part de q₀ = 0 (point sans bord, euclidien). Aucune mention de a → ∞ comme raccord, de l'infini futur, ni de Penrose. Rien sur (c) ni (d).

### Source 5 (Hartle, Hawking, Hertog) : cas (b) principalement ; rebond classique à rayon fini (pas à a = 0)

- p. 1 (abstract) : « If the classical late time histories are extended back, they may be singular or bounce at a finite radius. »
- p. 3 : « But for φ₀ > φ₀^s they bounce at a finite radius in the past. This is possible despite the singularity theorems because a scalar field and the cosmological constant violate the strong energy condition. Near the bounce the universe approaches a de Sitter state with radius ∼ (μφ₀)^{−1}. »
- p. 3 : « Indeed, there could be quantum mechanical transitions rather than classical ones across cosmological singularities that connect two classical regimes [10]. »
- p. 4 : « This leads us to predict that in a landscape potential, the most probable homogeneous history of the universe that is consistent with our data started in an unstable de Sitter like state near a broad saddle-point of V. »

Le rebond est un rebond classique à rayon minimal non nul d'un univers fermé Λ + scalaire. La possibilité de transitions quantiques « across cosmological singularities » est seulement évoquée et renvoyée à la référence [10] (Turok, Craps, Hertog, « From Big Crunch to Big Bang with AdS/CFT »), sans construction dans ce texte. Aucune mention de l'infini futur ni de Penrose.

### Source 6 (Brown, Dahlen) : cas (c), transitions entre vides de Sitter à l'intérieur d'un horizon

- p. 1 (abstract) : « Every de Sitter vacuum can transition to every other de Sitter vacuum despite any obstacle, despite intervening anti-de Sitter sinks, despite not being connected by an instanton. Eternal inflation populates the whole landscape. »
- p. 5 : « A fixed de Sitter background (with G_N = 0) is like a finite box in the sense that only a finite region of field needs to make the transition—inflation ensures that, once formed, a horizon-sized volume of C will classically endure. »
- p. 8 : « Following this modern interpretation, the transition path described above corresponds to the formation of a horizon-sized region of C, outside of which the field smoothly interpolates through B and back towards A. »
- p. 8 : « The scale factor is well behaved throughout and indeed the spacetime expands by less than an e-fold ».

Il s'agit de chemins lorentziens à action finie avec un champ scalaire dans un potentiel multipuits, pas d'un raccord a → ∞ puis a → 0. Le seul « crunch » mentionné est celui des régions AdS (p. 3 : « Once a field decays into AdS it can never leave—it can’t uptunnel out, and it experiences a crunch in finite time. »), et il n'est pas raccordé à un nouveau Big Bang. Aucune mention de l'infini futur ni de Penrose.

## Section Q2 : signe de l'amplitude semi-classique « sans bord » dans les sources 2, 3, 4

### Signe obtenu

**Source 2 : exp(−12π²/ħΛ).**
- p. 1 (abstract) : « we find that the dominant saddle contributes a semiclassical exponential factor which is precisely the inverse of the famous Hartle-Hawking result. »
- p. 6 : « This complex conjugate saddle point therefore can be (and, we shall show, is) relevant to the Lorentzian path integral, and gives a semiclassical exponential factor of e^{−12π²/(ħΛ)}, precisely the inverse of the Hartle-Hawking result. »
- p. 27, équation (48) : « G_nb[q₁; 0] ≈ e^{iπ/4} 3^{1/4} / (2(Λq₁ − 3)^{1/4}) e^{−12π²/(ħΛ) − i4π²√(Λ/3)(q₁ − 3/Λ)^{3/2}/ħ. [...] This concludes the explicit derivation of our result that the relevant saddle point contributes a weighting e^{−12π²/(ħΛ)}, the inverse of the Hartle-Hawking result. »

**Source 3 : exp(+12π²/ħΛ).**
- p. 5, équation (2.6) : « Ψ_NB(q₁) = e^{+12π²/ħΛ} / (Λq₁/3 − 1)^{1/4} cos[12π²/ħΛ (Λq₁/3 − 1)^{3/2} + 3π/4] [1 + O(ħ)]. (2.6) This is real and precisely equal to the no-boundary wave function familiar from its Euclidean formulation. »

**Source 4 : exp(−12π²/ħΛ).**
- p. 2 : « For the simplest case of a closed, Λ cosmology, the relevant saddle is a round Euclidean four-sphere, just as Hartle and Hawking claimed, but obtained via the conjugate continuation from de Sitter [7]. This inverts the semiclassical weighting, from e^{+12π²/(ħΛ)} to e^{−12π²/(ħΛ)}, agreeing with Vilenkin and representing a more physically intuitive ħ → 0 limit. »
- p. 3 : « G[q₁; 0] ∝ e^{−12π²/Λ − i4π²√(Λ/3)(q₁ − 3/Λ)^{3/2}}. As discussed in [7, 9], Picard-Lefschetz theory implies semiclassical suppression, in agreement with Vilenkin but not with Hartle and Hawking. »

### Raison du désaccord : le choix du contour d'intégration sur le lapse N

Les sources 2/4 intègrent le lapse sur la demi-droite réelle positive (0⁺, ∞) ; la source 3 l'intègre sur toute la droite réelle (−∞, +∞) en contournant N = 0 par en dessous. Les saddle points sélectionnés par Picard-Lefschetz sont alors différents (demi-plan supérieur pour 2/4, demi-plan inférieur pour 3).

Source 2, p. 22 : « Note that saddle points in the upper half plane lead to a e^{i2π²S₀} ∼ e^{−12π²/(ħΛ)}, while those in the lower half plane lead to e^{i2π²S₀} ∼ e^{+12π²/(ħΛ)}. » et p. 24 (légende Fig. 5) : « Only saddle point 1 in the upper right quadrant can be linked to the original integration contour via an upward flow, and this implies that the (orange-dashed) downward flow from this saddle point is the correct Lefschetz thimble along which the path integral should be performed. »

Source 2 donne aussi l'argument général, p. 5 : « The real part of the exponent Re[iS_cl/ħ], which determines the semiclassical factor in the quantum mechanical amplitude, necessarily decreases on the way down. Since the real part of the semiclassical exponent starts out zero on the real axis, it must be negative at any relevant saddle point. Such semiclassical factors, by this argument, can only suppress, and never enhance, a quantum mechanical amplitude. »

Source 3, p. 2 : « We consider a Lorentzian contour for the lapse integral that runs along the entire real axis and avoids the singularity at N = 0 by going below this point. [...] Our method resembles closely that of recent work of Feldbrugge et al. [18, 19] who also used Picard-Lefschetz theory in a Lorentzian framework but took the contour over N to be half-infinite. This leads to a significantly different result, which we will discuss. »

Source 3, p. 5 : « Independently of how the perturbation is taken to zero, we find that the two saddle points in the lower half complex N-plane contribute. »

Source 3, note 5, p. 5 (réponse directe à l'argument « suppression seulement » de la source 2) : « One may wonder how the Picard-Lefschetz prescription can select exponentially enhanced saddle point contributions. Note however that our original contour passes just below the origin, where the real part of the exponent of the integrand behaves as +1/ε. By a downwards flow from here we are led to the usual no-boundary saddle points, which are exponentially enhanced to leading order in ħ. By contrast in the analysis of [18] the real part of the exponent of the integrand is zero, so one can only flow to exponentially suppressed saddles. »

Source 3, p. 11 : « Our results differ significantly from those of [18, 19] who also used Picard-Lefschetz theory to evaluate a Lorentzian minisuperspace path integral but with a contour for the lapse that runs over the positive real axis only. This choice of contour does not yield a solution of the Wheeler-DeWitt equation but rather a Green’s function. »

Source 4, p. 4, formule la même différence comme un choix de continuation au-dessus ou au-dessous d'une coupure dans le plan q complexe : « Continuing the q integral below the branch cut to q = 0, we obtain for the real part of the semiclassical exponent −6π² ∫_0^{q_B} √(1 − ρq/3). For a cosmological constant ρ(q) = Λ, we obtain −12π²/Λ. Continuing above the branch cut yields +12π²/Λ, Hartle and Hawking’s result, which is inconsistent with Picard-Lefschetz theory. »

### Les auteurs de la source 3 disent-ils que le contour est fixé par un principe ou que c'est un choix ?

Ils présentent leur contour comme motivé par des considérations physiques, et qualifient explicitement la restriction N > 0 de leurs adversaires de « choix, non nécessité » ; ils laissent ouverte l'exploration des deux options :

- p. 4 : « Motivated by physical considerations which will become clear below we take the integral (2.3) over the lapse along the contour C = (−∞, +∞)↓ indicated in Figure 1 by the black curve. »
- p. 12 : « None of these statements are at variance with the contribution of Teitelboim [12] (the apparent motivation for the choice N > 0 in [18, 19]), who suggested taking positive lapse purely by way of analogy to the familiar causal structure of Minkowski space time, but also acknowledged that this is a choice, not a necessity, thus leaving full freedom to explore the consequences of either half-infinite or infinite contours. »
- p. 11 à 12 : « Note that the direction of the lapse integration is not directly related to the observed arrows of time [...] Reversing the sign of the lapse merely reverses the direction of parametrization of these curves without physical effect. »
- p. 2 (introduction) : la formulation euclidienne traditionnelle est décrite comme « a Euclidean path integral taken along an appropriate complex contour, whose choice is constrained by certain reasonable physical considerations [2]. »

Le critère qu'ils invoquent en faveur de leur contour : obtenir une solution de Wheeler-DeWitt (p. 6 : « Since the right-hand side vanishes, we see that our solution Ψ indeed satisfies the Wheeler-DeWitt equation. If by contrast we had taken C to be a half-infinite contour, we would have found a delta function on the right-hand side in (2.8) producing a Green’s function G of the Wheeler-DeWitt equation rather than a genuine solution Ψ [18]. ») et retrouver la QFT en espace courbe (p. 11 : « This choice fails to recover quantum field theory in curved space time [2], and thus fails to provide a reasonable physical basis for a predictive framework for cosmology. »). Ils ne disent nulle part que leur contour est imposé par un principe unique ; le mot employé pour leur propre contour est « our choice of contour » (p. 6 : « Finally we verify that our choice of contour yields a solution to the Wheeler-DeWitt equation. »).

### Les auteurs de la source 4 disent-ils qu'un autre contour peut éviter leur conclusion ?

Non. Ils affirment au contraire qu'aucun contour n'évite les perturbations non supprimées, en renvoyant la démonstration à un article compagnon :

- p. 1 : « There are three available options: i) integrate N_E over a half-range, should that integral converge; ii) leave the lapse real and Lorentzian, or iii) deform the lapse integral onto some other complex contour. We consider (and rule out) all three options. »
- p. 2 : « Finally, we consider alternative complex contours for the lapse integral, including the recent proposal of Diaz Dorronsoro et al. [8]. In a longer, companion paper [9] we show that neither this, nor any other choice of contour avoids the problem of unsuppressed perturbations. We also study nonlinear back reaction numerically, showing it does not qualitatively alter our conclusions. »
- p. 2 : « Thus we revert to the Lorentzian path integral which, taken either over real 0⁺ < N < ∞, or −∞ < N < ∞, yields a conditionally convergent, meaningful result. »
- p. 5 : « We conclude that the problem of unbounded perturbations, at small wavelengths, is unavoidable. »

Note d'audit : la démonstration pour les contours alternatifs (dont celui de la source 3) n'est pas contenue dans la source 4 ; elle est annoncée dans la référence [9] (« J. Feldbrugge, J.-L. Lehners, and N. Turok, arXiv eprint, (2017) », p. 5), qui ne fait pas partie du corpus audité. La source 4 contient en revanche le théorème général (p. 4 et 5) sur le signe positif de l'exposant des perturbations pour tout fluide adiabatique dont le point de rebond q_B est contourné par en dessous.

## Section Q3 : destin des perturbations à travers la transition ou l'origine ; information, mémoire, flèches du temps

### Source 1 (Gielen, Turok) : transmission sans changement (fréquence positive → fréquence positive, pas de création de particules)

- p. 1 : « For conformal matter, we show there is no quantum creation of scalar density perturbations or gravitational waves across the bounce, indicating a well-defined vacuum. For theories with nontrivial running and/or soft breaking terms, one would find finite particle production. »
- p. 3 : « The modes appearing in the propagator have remarkable properties: an incoming positive-frequency mode continues to an outgoing positive-frequency mode, without even a phase shift. »
- p. 3 à 4 : « If we normalize the positive- and negative-frequency modes at negative a, their norm is preserved as we pass to positive a, again by Schwarz reflection through Re(a) = 0. »
- p. 5 : « There is no mixing of positive and negative frequencies, and, hence, no particle production. The full functions h_T^{(2)} and ψ^{(2)} decay exponentially for negative imaginary η. These properties extend to all orders in the perturbation expansion, unambiguously defining nonlinear positive frequency modes [9]. »

Caveat exprimé par les auteurs, p. 5 : « On the real η-axis, ψ^{(2)} does not decay at large |η|, indicating a breakdown of perturbation theory. [...] The radiation fluid is governed by nonlinear dynamics, eventually leading to shocks. »

Information / mémoire / flèches du temps : la source parle d'une « unitary description » (p. 1 : « This picture provides a natural, unitary description of quantum mechanical evolution across a cosmological bounce. ») et de la conservation de la norme de DeWitt (p. 3 à 4). Elle n'emploie pas les mots « information », « memory » ni « arrow of time ». Sur la mémoire effacée ou les flèches divergentes : la source ne dit rien. Une transmission unitaire sans création de particules implique, selon les mots de la source, un vide « bien défini » des deux côtés, ce que l'on peut lire comme une transmission d'état, mais la source ne formule pas elle-même d'énoncé sur l'information.

### Source 2 (Feldbrugge, Lehners, Turok) : la source ne dit rien de quantitatif sur les perturbations

Le modèle est le minisuperespace sans ondes gravitationnelles, p. 14 : « This is a gross simplification of the original theory – we no longer have propagating gravitational waves – but we retain a dynamical scale factor a(t) as well as diffeomorphism invariance in the timelike coordinate t ». La discussion (p. 39) n'annonce que la convergence de la mesure : « In particular, the fluctuations about our homogeneous, isotropic but sometimes complex saddle point solutions, will themselves possess quadratic, complex actions. It is clear that Picard-Lefschetz theory applies rather trivially to this case and always yields a convergent measure on the space of field fluctuations. » Sur le caractère supprimé ou amplifié des perturbations : la source ne dit rien. Sur l'information, la mémoire, les flèches du temps : la source ne dit rien (elle mentionne seulement, p. 39, que « two of us have recently shown how cosmological time can emerge [30] », sans contenu dans ce texte).

### Source 3 (Díaz Dorronsoro et al.) : supprimées (gaussiennes) ; flèches du temps qui se renversent autour du rebond

- p. 1 (abstract) : « predicts an ensemble of asymptotically classical, inflationary universes with nearly-Gaussian fluctuations and with a smooth semiclassical origin. »
- p. 10, équation (3.23) : « Ψ_NB(q₁, φ_{l,1}) ∼ e^{4π²/ħH²} e^{−π² l(l+1)(l+2) φ²_{l,1}/ħH²} cos[...]. »
- p. 11 : « This result qualitatively generalizes to scalar and tensor perturbations around all classical backgrounds in both ensembles. Hence we recover the well-known Gaussian behaviour of the wave function of perturbations in the no-boundary state [17, 31]. »
- p. 10 : « Eq. (3.19) shows that small homogeneous perturbations around the de Sitter saddle points are suppressed. »

Flèches du temps : p. 11 : « The no-boundary condition of regularity on the perturbations implies that the Lorentzian perturbation histories exhibit growth in the direction of expansion in the classical backgrounds in both ensembles predicted by the wave function. This means that even if one were to connect both ensembles quantum mechanically, the physical arrows of time would reverse around the bounce [32, 33]. This is in sharp contrast with the causality in ekpyrotic cosmology where the fluctuation arrow of time points in the same direction across the entire spacetime. »

p. 12 : « these physical arrows arise because the no-boundary wave function predicts that fluctuations are small when the universe was small. »

Information : le seul énoncé est holographique, p. 12 : « The reversal of the physical arrows of time in the histories predicted by the no-boundary wave function means it is conceivable that a single dual defined at future spacelike infinity indeed encodes all physical correlations. » Sur une information qui passerait d'un côté à l'autre ou une mémoire effacée : la source ne dit rien explicitement ; elle dit que les flèches divergent du rebond.

### Source 4 (Feldbrugge, Lehners, Turok) : amplifiées (gaussienne inverse, « out of control »), mode de fréquence positive « faux »

- p. 1 (abstract) : « Unfortunately, the outcome is that primordial tensor (gravitational wave) fluctuations are unsuppressed. We prove a general theorem to this effect, in a wide class of theories. »
- p. 2 : « However, as a result of the abovementioned complex-conjugate nature of the background, the path integral yields an inverse Gaussian weighting for the final perturbation. Hence, large perturbations are favored and the theory is out of control. »
- p. 4 : « G_φ[φ₁; 0] ∝ e^{l(l+1)(l+2)φ₁²/(2ħH²)} × phase corresponding to an inverse Gaussian distribution. »
- p. 4 : « This is the late time expansion of the “positive frequency” mode function, confirming that the no boundary condition selects the “wrong” mode function as compared to the adiabatic ground state. »
- p. 4 : « Taking the continuation implied by Picard-Lefschetz theory for the background, the Euclidean action for the perturbations has the “wrong” sign. We can still impose regularity of the modes in the Euclidean region, but the resulting semiclassical weighting factor will inherit the wrong sign. »

Comparaison explicite avec le résultat standard en de Sitter plat (p. 2, éq. (2)) : « G^{(2)}_φ[φ₁] ∝ e^{−k³φ₁²/(2H²) + i k²φ₁²/(2H²η₁)}. [...] we recover the familiar result of a scale-invariant power spectrum for φ₁. » Dans ce calcul standard, la solution point-selle est « “negative frequency” at early times » (p. 2), tandis que la condition sans bord sélectionne la fréquence positive (p. 4), d'où l'inversion du signe.

Information / mémoire / flèches du temps : la source ne dit rien.

### Source 5 (Hartle, Hawking, Hertog) : fluctuations minimales au rebond, croissantes de part et d'autre ; flèche thermodynamique divergente ; pas d'impact causal d'un côté sur l'autre

- p. 3 : « For the universes that bounce at a minimum radius it seems likely that the NBWF will predict that fluctuations away from homogeneity and isotropy will be at a minimum at the bounce and grow away from the bounce for at least a while on either side (cf. [7]). This means that the thermodynamic arrow of time is likely to point away from the bounce on either side of it. Events on one side are therefore unlikely to have a causal impact on the other and have much explanatory value. This is very different from the causality in pre-big bang universes where the arrow of time points in one direction throughout the spacetime. »
- p. 3 : « However, the reality of the NBWF implies the ensemble of allowed classical histories is time symmetric. For every history in this ensemble, its time reverse is also a member. »

Sur le caractère gaussien : la source ne traite pas explicitement le spectre des perturbations ; la section « Inhomogeneities » (p. 4) dit : « In this paper we have discussed homogeneous universes only. However, one can also consider inhomogeneous perturbations. It appears that the volume weighting can overcome the gradient action for very long wavelength perturbations that leave the horizon while (6) is satisfied. This suggests the NBWF with volume weighting will predict a universe that is very inhomogeneous on very large scales. » Le mot « supprimée » n'apparaît pas ; la source ne dit rien de quantitatif sur le spectre.

Information : la source ne parle pas d'« information » ni de « mémoire », mais l'énoncé sur l'absence d'impact causal d'un côté sur l'autre (p. 3) est une formulation explicite d'une divergence des flèches du temps à partir du rebond.

### Source 6 (Brown, Dahlen) : mémoire des conditions initiales effacée ; rien sur les perturbations

- p. 2 : « this would mean that the combination of exponential expansion of the vacua and quantum transitions between the vacua would erase the memory of the starting point. The landscape would have no hair. »
- p. 9 : « The important thing, however, is that the rate is non-zero: a non-zero rate, no matter how small, is sufficient to erase memory of initial conditions and populate the whole landscape. »
- p. 4 : « the gravitational degrees of freedom will decohere the different branches of the wavefunction with different values of φ and in so doing play the role of the measurement apparatus. »
- p. 5 : « field theory on the causal patch of de Sitter is just the field theory of a horizon-sized box (with a finite temperature and a position-dependent Hamiltonian [7]) » ; et : « The crucial feature of de Sitter is that only a finite volume needs to transition, not that there is a nonzero temperature. »

Sur le destin des perturbations (ondes gravitationnelles, anisotropies) à travers les transitions : la source ne dit rien. Sur les flèches du temps : la source ne dit rien. Sur l'information : la source dit que la mémoire des conditions initiales est effacée (au sens d'un attracteur de l'inflation éternelle, p. 2 : « starting in any eternally inflating state the Universe would be driven towards the same attractor »).
